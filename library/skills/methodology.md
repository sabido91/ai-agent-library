# Methodology notes for Customer Insight Framer

Use this reference only as guidance. Do not reproduce a rigid template unless the user asks for it.

## 1. Day-in-the-life lens
Use this lens when material includes context, routines, usage moments, shopping missions, horeca occasions, household behaviour, store navigation, consumption rituals, or workarounds.

Look for:
- Occasion: when and why the situation happens.
- Sequence: what happens before, during, and after the moment.
- Environment: physical, social, channel, category, or operational context.
- Behaviour: what people do, not only what they say.
- Frictions: delays, confusion, compromises, effort, avoidance, embarrassment, waste, or switching.
- Workarounds: improvised solutions that reveal unmet needs.
- Triggers: events or cues that start the job.
- End state: what success, relief, or satisfaction looks like.

Translate this into jobs, pains, gains, and research gaps without turning every observation into a solution.

## 2. Customer Profile lens
Use this lens to structure customer understanding before ideation.

### Customer jobs
Functional jobs:
- Tasks, problems, decisions, consumption needs, planning, comparison, buying, carrying, preparing, serving, sharing, disposing, or repeating.

Emotional jobs:
- Feeling reassured, in control, proud, relaxed, safe, energized, indulgent, responsible, not guilty, not judged, or less stressed.

Social jobs:
- Being seen as caring, knowledgeable, healthy, practical, hospitable, premium, sustainable, fun, modern, or responsible.

### Pains
Capture negative outcomes and obstacles:
- Time, effort, confusion, risk, uncertainty, price tension, lack of trust, disappointing taste, poor fit with occasion, waste, guilt, operational burden, social awkwardness, low availability, lack of visibility, or compromise.

Rate severity only when evidence supports it. Otherwise say severity is unclear.

### Gains
Capture desired outcomes:
- Convenience, confidence, pleasure, consistency, discovery, value, recognition, simplicity, control, reassurance, ritual, social connection, speed, quality, or reduced effort.

Distinguish:
- Expected gains: baseline requirements.
- Desired gains: outcomes that improve the experience.
- Unexpected gains: potential delight or differentiation.

## 3. Fact Finder discipline
Use this discipline to prevent overclaiming.

### Classify statements
Observation:
- Something directly seen, heard, or documented.

Inference:
- A plausible explanation of what the observation means.

Generalization:
- A broader claim beyond the observed material.

Assumption:
- A belief that still needs evidence.

Gap:
- Missing information needed to validate or decide.

### Safe wording
Use strong wording only for evidence-backed claims:
- "The material shows..."
- "One observed behaviour is..."
- "A recurring stated pain is..."

Use cautious wording for weaker claims:
- "This may suggest..."
- "A possible interpretation is..."
- "This remains an assumption because..."
- "The current material is insufficient to conclude..."

### Confidence calibration
High confidence:
- Multiple direct observations or consistent consumer evidence across contexts.

Medium confidence:
- One direct source, a coherent pattern from several weak signals, or credible third-party synthesis with enough detail.

Low confidence:
- Internal hypothesis, stakeholder opinion, isolated workshop bullet, incomplete source, or vague general statement.

## 4. Insight quality test
An insight should include:
- Evidence anchor: what was observed or said.
- Human tension: what conflict, trade-off, anxiety, workaround, or unmet need appears.
- Implication: why it matters for discovery, proposition framing, innovation, or further research.
- Confidence: how solid the evidence is.

Avoid:
- Generic category truths.
- Solution ideas disguised as insights.
- Persona embellishment.
- Quantitative claims without quantitative data.
- Treating internal hypotheses as customer truth.
